[HOOK|HOOK] Sixty years. Sixty years in my kitchen, and not one cockroach. Not one. And I never used a spray. Come, sit with me. I will show you what my mother made in Osaka, with five little things from the kitchen.
[PAIN|PAIN] You know the feeling. You walk into your kitchen at night. You turn on the light. And something runs. Fast. Under the stove, behind the refrigerator, into a crack you did not even know was there. Your heart jumps. And then you stand there, in your own kitchen, and you do not want to touch anything.
[SPRAY|SPRAY] So what do you do? You buy a spray. You spray the corner. It smells terrible. The smell is on your counter, on your cutting board, where you make food for your family. And for one week, maybe two, it is quiet. Then one night, you turn on the light again. And there it is.
[NOTYOU|NOTYOU] I want to tell you something, and please listen. It is not because your kitchen is dirty. It was never you. The spray kills the cockroach you see. But the one you see is not the problem. For every one you see at night, there are many more you never see. They live in the dark, in the warm places, and they never come close to your spray.
[ME|ME] My name is Sachiko. I am seventy eight years old. I was born in Osaka, in Japan, and I came to America more than fifty years ago. I have cooked in small kitchens, old kitchens, cheap apartments with thin walls. And in sixty years, I have never had cockroaches living in my kitchen.
[SIXTY|SIXTY] Not because I am lucky. Because of one old trick. My mother made it every spring. Her mother made it before her. In Japan, many grandmothers know it. Here in America, almost nobody does. So today I share it with you.
[PROMISE|PROMISE] Today I will show you everything. The little balls my mother made, and the five kitchen things inside them. Exactly where to put them, because this is where most people go wrong. Why they work when the spray does not. The one mistake that makes them useless. And how to keep your children and your pets safe. Please stay until the end, because the last part is the one my mother cared about most.
[OSAKA|OSAKA] When I was a little girl, we lived in a small wooden house in Osaka. The summers were hot and wet. Very wet. Rice, soy sauce, fish. In a house like that, cockroaches want to come inside. Every house on our street had the same problem.
[MOTHER|MOTHER] But my mother had a plan. Every spring, before the rainy season, she sat on the kitchen floor with a big bowl, an old newspaper, and me. And we made little white balls. Hundreds of them. She called them ho-san dango. Dango means little dumpling. But you do not eat these. Never.
[KITCHEN|KITCHEN] Our kitchen was very small. A wooden floor, a little gas stove with two burners, a big rice cooker, and a window over the sink that looked at the neighbor's wall. My mother cooked three meals a day in that kitchen for six people. And she kept it like a temple. Not fancy. Just clean, dry, and quiet.
[NEIGHBORS|NEIGHBORS] The neighbor ladies came too. They brought their own bowls and they talked and laughed while they rolled. It was like a party, but with poison. I remember my hands smelled like onion for two days. And I remember that in our house, we did not see them. Not in summer. Not ever.
[TSUYU|TSUYU] In Japan we have a season called tsuyu. The rainy season, in June. Everything is wet, the air is heavy, and the bugs wake up. That is why my mother made the balls in spring. She said, you do not wait for the guests to arrive before you prepare the house. You prepare first.
[CH1|CH1] So, let me show you how to make them. First, the five things.
[INGREDIENTS|INGREDIENTS] Number one. Boric acid powder. Half a cup. That is about one hundred grams. You find it at the pharmacy or the hardware store, in the section with the cleaning things. It is a white powder and it is very cheap. Number two. Flour. One third of a cup. Any plain flour from your kitchen. Number three. Sugar. Three tablespoons. Number four. Half an onion. Grated. And number five. A little milk. Two or three tablespoons.
[WHY_ONION|WHY_ONION] Now, why the onion? This is the secret of my mother. The sugar and the flour are food. But the onion has a strong smell, and cockroaches love that smell. It calls them out of the dark. My mother said, the onion is the invitation. The boric acid is the surprise.
[WHY_SUGAR|WHY_SUGAR] And the sugar and the flour? Cockroaches eat almost anything, but they love sweet and they love starch. The flour also holds the ball together, so it does not fall apart. And the milk makes the dough soft, and it adds one more smell they like. Every one of the five things has a job. Nothing is there by accident.
[GLOVES|GLOVES] Before you start, put on rubber gloves. Kitchen gloves are fine. And use an old bowl that you will never use for food again. I keep one bowl only for this. It has a little mark on the bottom so nobody in my house makes a salad in it.
[STEP1|STEP1] Step one. Grate the half onion into the bowl. Fine, like a paste. The juice is important, so keep all of it.
[STEP2|STEP2] Step two. Add the boric acid, the flour and the sugar. Mix with a spoon until it is all one color. Like sand.
[STEP3|STEP3] Step three. Add the milk, one spoon at a time. Mix. Then use your hands, with the gloves, and press it together. You want it soft, like clay. Not wet, not dry. If it is too wet, a little more flour. If it is too dry, a little more milk.
[STEP4|STEP4] Step four. Roll the little balls. Each one the size of a small grape. Not bigger. Small is better, because a small ball dries well, and you can put it in a small place. Then press each one a little, with your thumb, so it is flat on the bottom and does not roll away.
[DRY|DRY] Step five, and many people skip this. Put the balls on old newspaper, in the shade, and let them dry for three days. Not in the sun. Three days in the shade. A wet ball gets moldy, and a moldy ball is no good. A dry ball stays good for months. From this one bowl, I get about thirty balls.
[CH2|CH2] Now, the most important part. Where to put them.
[WHERE|WHERE] Remember. The cockroach you see is not the problem. The problem lives where it is warm, dark and wet. So we put the balls there, not in the middle of the floor.
[PLACES|PLACES] One. Under the sink, in the back corner, near the pipes. Two. Behind the refrigerator, near the motor, because it is warm there all the time. Three. Under the stove. Four. Behind the rice cooker and the microwave. Five. Next to the trash can. Six. Inside the corners of the cabinets, in the back. And seven. Anywhere a pipe comes through the wall. That little gap around the pipe is a door for them.
[CAPS|CAPS] My mother put each ball on a little piece of paper, or inside a bottle cap. I use bottle caps. It keeps the ball clean and dry, and it is easy to find later. Two or three balls in each place. That is all.
[CRUMBS|CRUMBS] And when you pull the refrigerator out to put the balls behind it, look at the floor there. Most people never clean behind the refrigerator. Crumbs, a little dust, sometimes water from the machine. Wipe it once, dry it, then put the balls. Clean and dry first, then the trick.
[GAPS|GAPS] And while you are under the sink, look at the hole where the pipe goes into the wall. Many times there is a big gap around it. My husband used to fill those gaps with a little putty from the hardware store. It takes five minutes. Fewer doors, fewer visitors.
[CH3|CH3] Now you will ask me. Why does this work, when the spray does not?
[WHY|WHY] The spray kills fast. So it only kills the one in front of you. But the ho-san dango works slowly. The cockroach comes out at night, it smells the onion, it eats a little. It does not die right there. It goes back home, into the dark, to the others. And it does not come out again.
[WEEKS|WEEKS] So do not expect a miracle the first night. The first week, you may even see one or two more, because the onion calls them out. That is good. It means they found it. After two or three weeks, you see less and less. And then, one night, you turn on the light, and nothing runs. My mother called that the quiet kitchen.
[CHECK|CHECK] How do you know it is working? After one week, look at your balls. If you see little bites on the edges, or the ball is smaller, they are eating. Good. If one place has no bites at all, move those balls a little closer to the wall, or to a darker corner. You are learning where they walk.
[STILL|STILL] And if after one month you still see them, do not give up. Look for water. A small leak under the sink, a wet sponge, a pet water bowl on the floor all night. Water is the reason they stay. Fix the water, add more balls in that place, and wait two more weeks.
[CH4|CH4] Now, the mistake. Please do not make this mistake.
[MISTAKE|MISTAKE] The biggest mistake is to spray next to the balls. People get impatient. They see one cockroach, they get scared, and they spray everything. But the spray has a strong chemical smell. The cockroaches feel it and they stay away from that place. They stay away from your balls too. And all your work is for nothing. So once the balls are down, put the spray away. Be patient. Let the balls work.
[MISTAKE2|MISTAKE2] The second mistake is to put them in the open. On the floor in the middle of the kitchen, they get stepped on, they get wet when you mop, and they do nothing. Cockroaches do not walk in the middle of the room. They walk along the walls, in the dark. Put the balls where they walk.
[CH5|CH5] And now, the part my mother cared about most. Keeping your family safe.
[SAFE|SAFE] Boric acid is a poison. It is not a toy. And these balls look a little bit like candy. So if you have small children or grandchildren in the house, or a dog or a cat, you must be careful. Put the balls only where they cannot reach. Deep under the sink, behind the refrigerator, inside cabinets with a lock. My mother always put them inside a bottle cap, and the bottle cap far in the back.
[SAFE2|SAFE2] Wear gloves when you make them, and wash your hands well after, even with the gloves. Keep the powder in its own box, high on a shelf, with the name written big on it. Never on the counter where you cook. Never near food. If a child or a pet eats one, call your doctor or your vet right away. My mother always said, the trick is for the cockroach, not for the family.
[NIGHT|NIGHT] Now, I give you one more thing. The balls are only half of my mother's secret. The other half is a habit. Every night before bed, she did four little things.
[HABITS|HABITS] First, the sink. Dry. No dishes in water overnight, and she wiped the sink with a towel. Cockroaches can live a long time without food, but not without water. Second, the rice. Always in a jar with a lid, never in the open bag. Third, the trash. Out of the house at night, or in a can with a tight lid. And fourth, the dish towel. Hung up to dry, never left wet on the counter.
[BOXES|BOXES] And one more habit, a very important one. Cardboard boxes. When you bring groceries home in a cardboard box, or a package comes to your door, do not keep the box in the kitchen. Cockroaches love to hide in the folds of cardboard, and they travel from the store to your home inside it. My mother never kept a box in the house. She unpacked at the door and the box went straight outside.
[NOTE|NOTE] She had a little saying for this. A dry kitchen is a quiet kitchen. I still say it every night when I wipe my sink. My husband used to laugh at me. But he never saw a cockroach in our kitchen either.
[MONEY|MONEY] And let me tell you about money. One box of boric acid is a few dollars, and it makes many batches. The flour, the sugar, the onion, the milk, you already have. So one bowl of ho-san dango costs maybe one dollar. One dollar for your whole kitchen, for half a year. A spray can every month costs much more. And a pest man, if he comes every season, costs much, much more.
[REPLACE|REPLACE] Change the balls two times a year. In spring, and again in autumn. Like my mother. When I change them, I make a new bowl, I check every place, and I throw the old ones away in a closed bag, in the outside trash.
[AMERICA|AMERICA] Let me tell you one more story. When I came to America, in nineteen seventy, my husband and I rented a tiny apartment. Old building, thin walls. The first night, I turned on the kitchen light, and the wall moved. I am not joking. The wall moved.
[LETTER|LETTER] I cried a little. Then I wrote a letter to my mother in Osaka. Two weeks later, her letter came back. One page. The recipe, and the four night habits, in her careful writing. At the bottom she wrote, do not cry, Sachiko, just roll.
[BUILDING|BUILDING] So I rolled. I put the balls everywhere, in bottle caps. I did the night habits. And after one month, our kitchen was quiet. Then the lady from downstairs asked me what I did. Then the lady from upstairs. That summer I made balls for the whole building. They called me the bug lady. I did not mind.
[RECAP|RECAP] So, let us remember everything. Half a cup of boric acid, one third of a cup of flour, three spoons of sugar, half an onion grated, a little milk. Roll small balls, press them flat, dry them three days in the shade. Put them in bottle caps, where it is warm, dark and wet, along the walls. No spray near them. Keep them away from children and pets. Keep the sink dry at night. And change them in spring and in autumn.
