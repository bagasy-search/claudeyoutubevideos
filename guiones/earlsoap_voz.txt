So what is it? It's a salt. A phosphate salt. The most common one has a long name, sodium tripolyphosphate. On the bag it might say that, or it might just say sodium phosphate, or something with the word phosphate in it.
Now, I'm not going to stand here and tell you it's poison. It's allowed. It's used in a lot of foods. But here's what it does to a shrimp, and here's why it's in there.
When a shrimp sits in that phosphate solution, it soaks up water and holds on to it. The shrimp swells up. It looks plump and shiny and pretty in the case. And it weighs more.
And you pay by the pound. So part of what you paid for in that bag is water. Water that was put there on purpose.

Folks ask me all the time, Earl, can't I just rinse it off? No sir. You can rinse that shrimp under the faucet all day long. The soak isn't on the outside. It's inside the meat. It went in, and the only way it's coming out is in your hot pan. So don't waste your water. Just buy better shrimp.
There's a second thing they sometimes add, and this one you should know about too. A sulfite, usually sodium bisulfite. They'll tell you it's to keep the shrimp moist in the freezer, and there's a little truth to that. But I froze shrimp on my boat for thirty-one years without a speck of powder, and they were never dry. Cold, fast, and clean. That's all a shrimp needs.
