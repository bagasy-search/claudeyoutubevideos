Let's talk about why oil is the wrong answer.
A chicken thigh with the skin on is already carrying plenty of fat. It's right under the skin, a layer of it. If you can melt that fat out, slowly, it collects in the pan and fries the skin from underneath. That's how you get skin that's thin and crackling, like a potato chip.
But when you put the thigh into a hot pan full of oil, the outside of the skin seizes up and browns in a minute. The fat underneath doesn't have time to melt out. So it stays trapped under a brown skin, and you get skin that looks golden but it's flabby and chewy and greasy underneath. Then you've got extra oil in the pan on top of all the chicken fat. That's the grease that spits all over your stove.
Starting in a cold pan does the opposite. As the pan slowly heats up, the fat under the skin melts out a little at a time. The skin gets thinner and thinner as it renders. And then, once all that fat's in the pan, the skin fries in it and turns deep gold and crisp. It just takes patience. Twenty minutes of it.
