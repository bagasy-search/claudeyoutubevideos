[N25 | a glass bowl of sliced carrots shining in a red-orange dressing with rings of onion and green pepper] Number twenty-five. Copper pennies. You slice two pounds of carrots into rounds, about like a coin, and you boil them just until they are tender but still have a little bite, five or six minutes. Drain them and run cold water over them so they stop cooking.
[N25 | she stirs the dressing in a saucepan] Then you make the dressing. One can of tomato soup, a half cup of oil, three quarters of a cup of vinegar, one cup of sugar, a teaspoon of mustard and a teaspoon of Worcestershire. Bring it to a boil, pour it hot over the carrots with a sliced onion and a green pepper, and put it in the refrigerator overnight. The next day they shine like new pennies. That is where the name comes from.
[N24 | thin slices of cucumber in a white sour cream dressing with dill, in a small glass bowl] Number twenty-four. Creamed cucumbers. Slice two cucumbers thin, sprinkle them with a teaspoon of salt, and let them sit thirty minutes.
[N24 | an old hand squeezing the water out of the cucumber slices over the sink] Now here is the little thing. You squeeze them. Then stir in half a cup of sour cream, a tablespoon of vinegar, a teaspoon of sugar, a little dill and a thin slice of onion. Cold and simple, and gone in ten minutes.
[N23 | a jar of deep purple pickled beets with whole peeled eggs turned magenta beside them] Number twenty-three. Pickled beets, and the red beet eggs that go with them. [N23 | an egg cut in half showing a magenta ring around a yellow yolk] And then you drop in peeled hard boiled eggs and you leave them in the refrigerator for a day or two. [N22 | leaf lettuce and green onions in a bowl, hot bacon dressing being poured over it, the lettuce wilting] Number twenty-two. Wilted lettuce. [N22 | she pours the hot dressing from the skillet over the lettuce] Into the drippings go a quarter cup of vinegar, a tablespoon of sugar and two tablespoons of water. Bring it to a bubble and pour it right over the lettuce and green onions while it is hot. It wilts down in front of your eyes. You have to eat it right away, so we always made it last, right before the line opened.
[N21 | a big bowl of shredded cabbage with onion and green pepper, a saucepan of hot dressing next to it] Number twenty-one. Twenty-four hour slaw. This is the coleslaw you make on Saturday for Sunday, and it has no mayonnaise at all, so it can sit out on a picnic table and nobody worries.
[N21 | she pours the hot vinegar dressing over the cabbage] Shred one head of cabbage, and add a chopped onion and a green pepper. Pour it hot over the cabbage, cover it, and leave it in the refrigerator a whole day. It gets better every hour.
[N20 | a glass bowl of green beans, yellow wax beans and dark red kidney beans with rings of red onion] Number twenty. Three bean salad. One can of green beans, one can of yellow wax beans, one can of kidney beans, all drained and rinsed, and a little red onion sliced thin.
[N20 | she whisks the dressing in a measuring cup] The dressing is half a cup of sugar, two thirds of a cup of vinegar, a third of a cup of oil, salt and pepper. Make it the night before. [N19 | a bowl of bright green peas with cubes of cheddar, chopped egg and bacon in a creamy dressing] Number nineteen. Pea salad. And here is the thing everybody gets wrong. You do not cook the peas. [N19 | she folds the mayonnaise into the peas with a wooden spoon] Then add a cup of cheddar cut in little cubes, a quarter cup of chopped onion, two chopped hard boiled eggs and half a cup of mayonnaise. [N18 | a big bowl of elbow macaroni salad with celery, sweet pickle and egg, a paper plate beside it] Number eighteen. Church basement macaroni salad. [N18 | she pours a little pickle juice from the jar into the dressing] And the dressing is where the church ladies were smart. One cup of mayonnaise, two tablespoons of yellow mustard, a tablespoon of sugar, and two tablespoons of the juice from the pickle jar. That pickle juice is the secret. Mix it the night before, because the macaroni drinks up the dressing, and in the morning stir in a little more mayonnaise so it is creamy again.
[N17 | a tall glass bowl showing layers: lettuce, celery, peas, white mayonnaise, yellow cheese and bacon on top] Number seventeen. Seven layer salad. You make this one in a glass bowl, so everybody can see the layers. Lettuce in the bottom, then chopped celery, then a little onion, then a bag of frozen peas right out of the freezer.
[N17 | an old hand spreading mayonnaise all the way to the edges of the glass bowl with a spatula] And now the important part. You spread two cups of mayonnaise over the top, all the way to the glass, like you are frosting a cake. That seals it, so the lettuce stays crisp. Sprinkle a tablespoon of sugar over the mayonnaise, then two cups of shredded cheddar and a pound of bacon, crumbled. Cover it and refrigerate it overnight. You don't stir it, honey. You dig down with the spoon so everybody gets a little of each layer.
[N16 | warm red potato slices with bacon and onion in a tangy glossy dressing, steam rising] Number sixteen. Hot German potato salad. [N16 | onion cooking in bacon drippings in a skillet, vinegar being poured in] Fry six slices of bacon and cook a chopped onion in the drippings. Then stir in a tablespoon of flour, a third of a cup of vinegar, two tablespoons of sugar and half a cup of water, and let it bubble until it thickens. Pour it over the warm potatoes with the bacon. It should taste sweet and sour at the same time, and it should be served warm.

[N13 | a bowl of white creamy fruit salad with mandarin oranges, pineapple, coconut and little marshmallows] Number thirteen. Five cup salad. [N5 | a casserole of green beans in a creamy mushroom sauce topped with crispy fried onions] Number five. Green bean casserole.
