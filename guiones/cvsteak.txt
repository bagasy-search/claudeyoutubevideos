A steak cooks from the outside in. When you put a cold steak in a screaming hot pan, the outside heats up fast, and the heat moves inward slowly, a little at a time. By the time the very middle reaches medium-rare, the outside layers have been sitting at a much higher temperature for minutes. They're well-done. That's the gray band. The thicker the steak, the worse it gets, because the heat has further to travel.
So here's what we do instead. We turn it around. We cook the steak low and slow first, in a two-hundred-and-fifty-degree oven, so the heat creeps in gently and the whole steak, edge to edge, comes up to the same temperature.
