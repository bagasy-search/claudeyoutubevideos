Now let me tell you what actually changes the price, because two machines of the same model can sell for very different amounts.
First, does it run? Turn the hand wheel slowly toward you. Does the needle go up and down smoothly? Does it feel free, or stuck? [emphatically] A machine that turns freely is worth more, even if you don't plug it in.
And please, don't plug in an old machine until you look at the cord. Old wiring gets dry and cracked. If the rubber on the cord or the foot pedal is crumbling, don't plug it in. Second, the case. For the small machines, the original case matters a lot. A Featherweight without its case loses a good chunk of its value. Check that the latches work and the handle is there.
Third, the little parts. Look inside the case or the cabinet drawers for the accessories. The bobbin case, extra bobbins, the foot pedal, the instruction book, and a little box of attachments. Fourth, the paint and the decals. A machine that's been kept covered, with shiny black paint and bright gold decals, looks like money. A machine with scratches, rust and worn decals looks like work.
