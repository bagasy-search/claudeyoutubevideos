Now, here's the part I promised you. The thing almost everybody uses that doesn't keep anything out. Chicken wire. I know, it's called chicken wire. But chicken wire keeps chickens in. It doesn't keep predators out. A raccoon can tear it, reach through it, or pull it apart at the seams. And a mink goes right through those big holes like they aren't there.
What keeps them out is hardware cloth. It's a heavy welded wire with small square openings. I use the half inch. You can't reach through it, you can't squeeze through it, and you can't tear it with your hands. It costs more, but it's the only thing I'll put on a coop now.
So here's what I did. First, I pulled off that old chicken wire. Then I covered every opening in the coop with half inch hardware cloth. The windows, the vents up high, every gap. Screwed down with washers, not stapled. A raccoon can pull out staples.
Then I looked at the run. Around the bottom of the run, I dug a little trench and buried hardware cloth about a foot deep, bent outward, like an apron. Anything that digs at the fence hits wire and gives up.
I checked my latches. Every door on my coop has a two-step latch. A spring clip through the bolt. A raccoon can work a simple hook, but two steps they can't figure. And I made sure the little hen door gets closed every single night at dusk, when the hens go to roost. That's when most of the trouble starts.
And then I did one more thing. I bought a little trail camera, and I strapped it to a post facing that vent.
So here's what it cost me. A roll of half inch hardware cloth, about forty-five dollars at my feed store. A box of screws and washers, eight dollars. And the trail camera, about forty dollars. All together, just under a hundred dollars.
Now, I know that's money. But I lost three good laying hens. And a hundred dollars of wire is cheaper than losing three more next week.
And here's what I won't spend money on. Those flashing lights you stick on a post that are supposed to scare predators away. Maybe they bother a fox for a week or two. Then the animals figure out it's just a light. A mink doesn't care. I'd rather put that money into wire.
Same with the bottles of predator scent, the stuff that smells like a coyote or a wolf. I tried it once, years ago. The raccoons didn't read the label.
