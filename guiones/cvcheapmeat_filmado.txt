[S03] Let's talk about why cheap meat is tough. Because once you understand it, you'll never be scared of it again.
[S03] The cheap cuts come from the parts of the animal that work the hardest. The shoulder, that's the chuck. The leg, that's the shank. A cow walks all day, so those muscles are strong, and they're held together with a lot of what the butchers call connective tissue. Collagen. It's that white, shiny, gristly stuff running through the meat. Raw, it's like rubber bands. You can't chew it.
[S03] But here's the thing. Collagen, if you cook it low and slow, with moisture around it, for a long time, it melts. It turns into gelatin. Gelatin is soft, and rich, and it coats every fiber of the meat. That's what makes a pot roast fall apart and feel juicy in your mouth. And here's the part most people don't know. A cheap cut has more collagen than a steak. So when it's cooked right, it's actually richer and more tender than an expensive cut. The expensive steak doesn't have the collagen to melt. It just dries out if you cook it long.
[S03] So why does it come out tough at home? Two reasons. Too hot, or too short. If you boil it hard, the meat fibers squeeze and dry out before the collagen has time to melt. And if you take it out after an hour or an hour and a half, the collagen hasn't melted yet. It's still rubber bands. People think it's tough because they cooked it too long. It's the opposite. Tough means it's not done yet.
[S04] So here's the secret ingredient. It's time. That's it. Time, low heat, and a lid you don't lift. Don Ernesto used to say, "The cheaper the meat, the more patience it costs. And patience is free."
