Rule one. Pick them and rinse them. Pour the dry beans out on a sheet pan and run your fingers through them. You're looking for little stones, dirt clods, shriveled or broken beans. I cracked a tooth on a pebble once, and believe me, you only do that once. Then rinse them in a colander.

Rule two. You don't need the overnight soak. Soaking only saves you twenty or thirty minutes of simmering. If you forgot to soak, you lost nothing. And if you did soak them, pour that water off and start with fresh.

Rule three. Salt at the start. One tablespoon of kosher salt for every pound of beans, right in the pot from the beginning. I know your grandmother told you salt keeps beans hard. It doesn't. It seasons them clear to the middle, and it keeps the skins tender.

Rule four. And listen close on this one, because this one is about keeping you safe, not about taste. Bring that pot to a hard, rolling boil and hold it there a full ten minutes.
