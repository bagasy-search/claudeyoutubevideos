So here is the recipe. This makes two dozen rolls. You will need one medium potato, peeled. One packet of active dry yeast, that is two and a quarter teaspoons. One cup of milk. A third of a cup of sugar. A third of a cup of butter. One egg. A teaspoon and a half of salt. And about four and a half to five cups of all-purpose flour.
First, boil the potato in about two cups of water until it is soft, fifteen or twenty minutes. Save the water. Mash the potato until it is smooth, and measure out half a cup. And measure half a cup of that potato water and let it cool.
Next, warm the milk with the butter and the sugar, just until the butter melts. Then let it cool down until it is warm, not hot. And here is how you know. Put a drop on the inside of your wrist. If it feels warm, like a baby's bath, it is right. If you have a thermometer, you want about a hundred and ten degrees.
Now sprinkle the yeast over the warm potato water, with a pinch of the sugar, and wait ten minutes. It should get foamy, like the head on a root beer. If it doesn't foam, your yeast is old, and you stop right there and get new yeast. That ten minutes saves you a whole morning.
In your big bowl, stir together the foamy yeast, the warm milk, the egg, the half cup of mashed potato and the salt. Then add the flour one cup at a time, stirring with a wooden spoon, until you have a soft dough that pulls away from the bowl.
And let me tell you how to measure the flour, because this is where most heavy rolls come from. Don't scoop the cup down into the flour bag. That packs it in, and you get way too much. Spoon the flour into the cup, and level it off with the back of a knife. That is how Irene did it and that is how the recipe works.
Now at the church, back in the fifties, we used shortening most of the time, or lard from somebody's hogs, because it was cheap and the rolls came out very tender. Butter tastes better. I use butter now. Any of the three works the same in this recipe, the same amount.
Turn it out on a floured table and knead it for eight to ten minutes. Push it away with the heels of your hands, fold it back, turn it a quarter turn, and push again. And now here is the answer to the third thing, the one about no time on a holiday. You don't have to bake these today. Put the dough in a big buttered bowl, turn it over once so the top is buttered too, cover it tight, and put it in the refrigerator. Overnight, or for up to three days.

Now, five things that go wrong, and how to fix them. One. If the dough won't rise, it is the yeast, or it was too hot, or your kitchen is cold. Test your yeast first, and use the oven light.
Two. If your rolls spread out flat, the dough was too soft or the rolls were not shaped tight on top. Pull that top tight. Three. If they are heavy, you used too much flour. Next time stop adding flour while it is still a little tacky.
Four. If the middle is gummy, they were not baked long enough. Go by the color and the hollow knock, not the clock. And five. If they come out hard, you baked them too far apart, or you forgot the butter and the towel.
