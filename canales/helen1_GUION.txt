[HOOK] Most people freeze meat all wrong, and I can prove it with one grocery bag. Last spring a widow named Margaret set it on my counter. Inside were eleven pounds of pork chops, ground beef and chicken thighs. She paid sixty-three dollars for that meat in January. By April it was gray, covered in white frost, and it smelled like the inside of the freezer. She asked me if it was still good. I had to tell her the truth. Most of it was going in the trash.
[HOOK_B] Honey, it was not her fault. Nobody ever showed her. The meat was fine when it went in. The freezer did not ruin it. The way it was wrapped did, and where it sat, and how long. So today I am going to give you twenty-five freezer tricks from behind a butcher counter. Do them, and you will never throw out a pound of meat again. And for most of my customers on a fixed income, that is three or four hundred dollars a year back in the purse.
[PROMISE] We are going to count down from twenty-five. Number eight is a quarter on a cup of ice, and it can save your whole freezer while you are out of town. Number seven is going to start an argument at your dinner table, because yes, you can refreeze meat, and I will show you exactly when. And number one is the trick I tell every single person who cooks for one or two. It is worth more than the other twenty-four put together. A few butchers are going to be mad at me for that one. Let them.
[INTRO] You know me, I am Helen. Forty-one years behind this counter, in a little meat market in Iowa. My father cut meat here before me. Most of my customers are retired now, cooking for one or two, and I have watched what a freezer does to good meat for a very long time. So grab a pen, honey. Let us start.
[N25] Number twenty-five. Never put the store tray straight into the freezer. That foam tray and that thin plastic wrap are made for the ride home, not for the winter. The wrap lets air in, and in about three weeks you get those dry white spots on the edges. The fix takes ten seconds. Leave the meat in the tray if you want, but wrap the whole package again, tight, in plastic wrap, and then put it in a freezer bag. Two layers, no air. The store wrap is for the car, not for the freezer.
[N24] Number twenty-four is about air, because air is what burns your meat, not the cold. You press the bag flat with your hands, you zip it, and there is still a pocket of air in the corners. Here is the old trick. Fill a big bowl with cold water. Put the meat in a zipper freezer bag and lower it slowly into the water, with the top open. The water pushes every bit of air out. When the water reaches the zipper, seal it. It works almost like one of those machines, for nothing but a bowl of water.
[N23] Number twenty-three. Freeze ground beef flat, never in a brick. A one pound brick takes a whole day to thaw in the refrigerator, and the outside goes brown while the middle is still ice. Put one pound in a quart freezer bag, push the air out, and press it flat, about half an inch thick. Freeze it lying on a sheet pan. Now it thaws overnight, or in cold water in about half an hour. And flat bags stand up in the freezer like books on a shelf.
[N23_B] Now the part for people who cook for one. Before it freezes, take the handle of a wooden spoon and press two lines across the bag, like a plus sign. When it is frozen, you snap off one square. A quarter pound for one supper. The rest goes right back in. One pound, four suppers, nothing wasted.
[N22] Number twenty-two. Freeze it loose first, then bag it. If you throw six pork chops in one bag, they freeze into one block, and you have to thaw all six to eat one. Lay them on a sheet pan with a piece of parchment paper, not touching. Freeze them one or two hours, until they are hard. Then put them in the bag together. Now they come out one at a time, like cookies from a jar. I do this with chops, chicken thighs, meatballs, and sausage links.
[N21] Number twenty-one is where the money starts. The family pack is almost always cheaper by the pound. Chicken thighs in the big pack might be a dollar and forty a pound, and the little tray of four is over two dollars. People who live alone skip the big pack because they think they will waste it. Buy the big pack. The same day, at your kitchen table, split it into portions for one meal each. Cook for one, but buy for six.
[N20] Number twenty. Write on everything. I have seen a hundred freezers full of white bags nobody can name. Mystery meat is the most expensive meat in the freezer, because it is the meat you throw away. Keep a roll of masking tape and a marker right next to the freezer. Three things on every package: what it is, how much, and the date. Pork chops, two, October fifteenth. That is it.
[N19] Number nineteen. Your freezer is probably too warm. Meat keeps its best at zero degrees or colder. A lot of home freezers are set somewhere around ten degrees, and nobody knows it. Buy a little freezer thermometer at the hardware store, a few dollars. Put it in the middle, close the door, and look at it the next morning. If it reads above zero, turn the dial colder, one notch, and check again the next day.
[N19_B] One more thing about the dial. If the freezer is part of the refrigerator, do not turn it so cold that your milk freezes. One notch at a time, honey.
[N18] Number eighteen. Never put a warm pot in the freezer. I know, you made a big pot of chili and you want it put away. But a warm pot warms up everything around it. The meat next to it starts to thaw a little and then freezes again, and that makes big ice crystals that tear the meat. Put the chili in shallow containers, cool it in the refrigerator, and freeze it the next morning. Hot food goes in the refrigerator within two hours, not on the counter overnight.
[N17] Number seventeen. The freezer is a pause button, not a rewind button. It stops the clock. It does not turn it back. If ground beef sits in your refrigerator four days and then goes in the freezer, it comes out as four day old ground beef. Ground meat and chicken should go in the freezer within a day or two of buying them. Steaks and chops within three to five days. Freeze it the day you bring it home, and it comes out like the day you bought it.
[N16] Number sixteen is meat that seasons itself while it thaws. Put four chicken thighs in a freezer bag. Add two tablespoons of oil, a tablespoon of vinegar or lemon juice, two cloves of garlic, smashed, and a teaspoon of salt. Push the air out and freeze it flat. The night before, move it to the refrigerator. While it thaws, it marinates. The next day it goes straight in the pan. Supper is already seasoned, and you did the work three weeks ago.
[N15] Number fifteen is the sticker trick. Every meat counter marks down meat that is close to its date. Thirty, sometimes fifty percent off, with a bright sticker. Most people walk right past it. That meat is fine. It just has to be cooked or frozen today. So buy it, go home, and freeze it before you even put the groceries away. The sticker is not a warning. It is a sale with a deadline.
[N14] Number fourteen is for bacon. You open a pack, use three slices, and the rest goes slimy in the refrigerator. Take the whole pack, and roll the slices up in pairs, like little snails. Flash freeze them on a sheet pan, then bag them. Saturday morning you take out two rolls. Bacon has a lot of fat, and fat does not keep as long in the freezer, so use it within about a month.
[N13] Number thirteen will save you a lot of money. Freezer burn is not dangerous. Those gray, dry spots are just meat that lost its water. The government food safety people say it is safe to eat. It is only dry. So do not throw out the whole package. Cut off the dry parts with a knife before or after cooking. Or put that meat in something wet, a soup, a stew, chili, beans. Nobody at the table will ever know.
[N12] Number twelve. Know how long each meat keeps its best. Frozen at zero, meat stays safe for as long as it stays frozen. But the taste goes down. Ground meat, three to four months. Chops and steaks, four months to a year. A whole chicken, up to a year. Chicken pieces, about nine months. Sausage and bacon, one to two months. Cooked meat and leftovers, two to three months. I write this on a card and tape it inside the freezer door.
[N11] Number eleven. First in, first out. This is how every meat counter in the country works, and almost no home freezer does it. New meat goes to the back or to the bottom. Older meat comes to the front. And keep a list on the freezer door. When you take something out, cross it off. Then you never buy chicken when you already have three packs of chicken buried under the ice.
[N10] Number ten. Thaw it in the refrigerator, never on the counter. On the counter, the outside of the meat warms up into the danger zone while the middle is still frozen. Put it on a plate on the bottom shelf, so nothing drips on your food. Plan on about one day for every five pounds. A pound of chops, one night. If you forgot, put the sealed bag in a bowl of cold water and change the water every thirty minutes. A flat pound is ready in about an hour. Then cook it right away.
[N09] Number nine will surprise you. An empty freezer costs more to run than a full one. All that frozen meat holds the cold, so every time you open the door, a full freezer barely warms up. An empty one has to start over. If yours is half empty, fill the space with jugs of water, about three quarters full so they have room to freeze. And if the power goes out, a full freezer holds for about two days with the door closed. A half full one, about one day.
[N08] Number eight is the quarter on a cup of ice. If you go see the grandkids for a week and the power goes out, the meat can thaw and freeze again, and you would never know. Fill a small cup with water and freeze it. When it is solid, put a quarter on top and leave it in the freezer. When you come home, look at the coin. On top, everything stayed frozen. In the middle, it thawed partway. At the bottom, it all thawed. If the coin is at the bottom, honey, throw the meat out. It is not worth getting sick.
[N07] Number seven is the argument. Can you refreeze meat? Yes, if you thawed it in the refrigerator. Raw meat thawed in the refrigerator can go back in the freezer without cooking. It loses a little juice, but it is safe. If you thawed it in cold water or in the microwave, cook it first. Then you can freeze the cooked meat. And anything that sat on the counter more than two hours does not go back in at all.
[N06] Number six. You can cook it straight from frozen. People call me at the market at five in the afternoon in a panic because they forgot to take the chicken out. You do not need to thaw it. Frozen meat cooks safely. It just takes about half again as long. Chicken thighs that take thirty minutes take about forty-five. Frozen ground beef goes in the skillet with a lid, and you scrape off the cooked part as it browns. Use a meat thermometer. Chicken is done at one hundred and sixty-five degrees.
[N05] Number five. Keep a bone bag. Keep one gallon freezer bag in the door. Every bone, every chicken back, every wing tip, every end of an onion and stub of celery goes in. When the bag is full, it goes in a big pot with water for four hours. That is broth you did not pay for. Freeze the broth in a muffin pan, then pop out the frozen pucks into a bag. Each one is about half a cup. Store broth is two or three dollars a box.
[N04] Number four. Freeze the fat. When you cook bacon, pour the warm grease through a little strainer into an ice cube tray. Freeze it and keep the cubes in a bag. One cube fries two eggs, seasons a pot of beans, or turns a head of cabbage into supper. My mother would not throw away a spoonful of fat in her life, and she was right. It is flavor you already paid for.
[N03] Number three is the double wrap, the way we do it behind the counter. For a roast or anything you want to keep longer than a few months, wrap it tight in plastic wrap first, pressing it against the meat so there are no air pockets. Then wrap it again in freezer paper, the shiny side against the meat, and fold the ends like a present. Tape it and write on it. A roast wrapped like that is still good a year later.
[N02] Number two. Ask your butcher to cut it and wrap it. Most people do not know they can ask. A whole boneless pork loin might be under two dollars a pound. The same meat cut into chops on a little tray can be four dollars a pound. Buy the whole loin and ask the person behind the counter to cut it into one inch chops and wrap them in twos for the freezer. Most of us will do it for nothing, and gladly. That is our job.
[N01] And number one. The trick I tell every person who cooks for one or two. Do one freezer afternoon a month. The week the family packs go on sale, buy them all at once. Then spend one afternoon at your kitchen table with your sheet pans, your freezer bags, your bowl of water and your tape. Split everything into portions for one meal. Flat, labeled, air out. One afternoon, and the whole month of suppers is done.
[N01_B] Here is why it works. You stop going to the store hungry. You stop buying the little trays. You stop throwing out the half pack that went gray. My customers who do this tell me they go to the store half as often. And when you live on a fixed income, every trip you do not make is money you keep.
[TROUBLE] Now, a few problems I hear at the counter every week. If there are ice crystals inside the bag, there was air in it, or your freezer is going up and down. Check the thermometer and use the bowl of water. If your chops come out dry and tough, they were in there too long or wrapped in only one layer. Cook those slow, with a little broth and a lid. And if the meat smells sour after you thaw it, do not taste it to check. Throw it out. When in doubt, throw it out.
[MONEY] Let me do the arithmetic, because that is what my customers care about. The family pack instead of the little tray saves about a dollar a pound. A person who cooks for one eats maybe four pounds of meat a week. That is around four dollars a week. The sticker meat and the whole loin save another three or four. And the meat you stop throwing away, like Margaret's bag, is a few dollars more. Put it together and it is about ten dollars a week. Ten dollars a week is over five hundred dollars a year. That is a lot of groceries, honey.
[CLOSE] So here is your homework. Do not do all twenty-five this week. Do two. Buy a freezer thermometer, and next time you buy ground beef, freeze it flat in a bag. That is all. Then come back and tell me in the comments: what is the oldest thing in your freezer right now? Be honest. I will tell you if it is still good.
