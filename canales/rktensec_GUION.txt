Fix number one. Zero dollars. Use the deadbolt, every time, and know which part is weak.

A lot of homes have two locks on the front door. The knob or handle, and the deadbolt above it. The knob lock is usually the weakest lock on your house, no matter who made it. It's basically a spring latch. The deadbolt is the one that really holds the door.

Fix number two. About twenty-five dollars. Make the frame as strong as the lock.

This is the one I told you about at the beginning. The part most people never look at.

When somebody comes through a front door, nine times out of ten, the lock doesn't fail. The door frame does. The deadbolt goes into a metal plate on the frame called the strike plate, and that strike plate is usually held on with two short screws, about three-quarters of an inch long. Take those short screws out and put in three-inch screws that go all the way through the trim and into the solid wood framing of the house behind it. Do the same on the hinges, at least one screw per hinge. That's the three dollar screw I talk about all the time. If you want to go further, a heavy-duty strike plate with four long screws costs about fifteen to twenty-five dollars and makes that frame dramatically stronger.
