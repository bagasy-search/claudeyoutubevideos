Number two. And this one hurts, because smart people do it. I never force a breaker back on when it keeps tripping. And I never, ever swap it out for a bigger one so it'll "quit doing that."

See, a lot of folks think the breaker is the problem. They think it's some fussy little gadget that trips over nothing. And sure enough, it stops tripping. Congratulations. Now the thing that's going to burn up is the wire.

Because the breaker isn't there to protect your appliances. It's there to protect the wire inside your wall. Every wire can only handle so much current. The breaker is sized to trip before that wire gets hot. Look, You put a bigger breaker on it, and the wire is still the same skinny wire, inside the wall, right up against the wood framing and the drywall, where nobody can see it. And it heats up. When a breaker trips, it's talking to you. It's saying, "There's too much on here," or, "Something's wrong on here." Maybe you've got too many things on one circuit. Maybe one appliance is going bad. Maybe there's a nicked wire somewhere. But the answer is never to shut it up. Number four. I never take the ground off an outlet. You know, that third hole, the round one on the bottom. That little pin is the emergency exit.
