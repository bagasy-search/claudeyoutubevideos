[INTRO] You know me, I'm Ole. I cooked in the logging camps up in northern Minnesota, in a cook shack with a wood range the size of a pickup truck and a bean pot you could take a bath in. [RULES] Before the first supper, let me tell you the rules I lived by in those lean weeks, because the recipes don't work without them. Rule one. Shop from the list, not from the aisle. In camp the list was written on a board by the door, and if it wasn't on the board, it didn't come in on the sled. Same in your store. If it isn't on the list, it doesn't go in the cart.
[RULES] Rule two. Buy it whole and cut it yourself. A whole cabbage, a ten pound bag of potatoes, a block of cheese instead of the little bag that's already grated. Somebody at the factory is charging you to cut things up, and you have a knife at home.
[RULES] Rule three is the one that saved me. Meat flavors the pot, it doesn't fill the belly. Half a pound of sausage in a pot of potatoes feeds six people. The same pound of sausage on two plates feeds two. When the money ran out, the meat didn't disappear from my suppers. It just stopped being the whole plate.
[RULES] And rule four. Waste nothing. The bones went into broth. The stale bread became crumbs and French toast. Whatever vegetables were left on Friday went into the soup. I never threw out so much as an onion skin in those weeks, because the onion skin colors the broth.

[ONE] Supper number one. Potatoes, onion and eggs, all in one skillet. This was the supper I made the very first night the money ran out, because potatoes were the one thing we always had in the root cellar, sacks of them.
[ONE] Here's what you need for four plates. Three medium potatoes. One onion. Four eggs. Three tablespoons of bacon fat, or oil if you don't keep the drippings. Salt, pepper, a little paprika if you have it. That's the whole list.
[ONE] Now, most people get this wrong in one place. They slice the potatoes too thick and fry them hot, and they end up burned outside and raw in the middle. Slice them thin, about an eighth of an inch. Thin as a dime stood on its edge.
[ONE] Heat the fat in your biggest skillet over medium. Layer the potatoes in with the onion and half the salt. Then here's the old camp trick. Put a lid on it. Ten minutes covered. The potatoes steam in their own water and go tender all the way through.
[ONE] Then take the lid off, turn them, and give them ten more minutes so they brown in spots. Season them. Make four little hollows with the back of your spoon, crack an egg in each one, cover it again, four or five minutes until the whites set.
[ONE] Lars, my second cook, used to put a shake of hot sauce on top and call it a Swedish breakfast for supper. What does it cost today? About two dollars and sixty cents for the whole skillet. Four plates. That's sixty-five cents a plate. Cover first, brown after.
[ONE] And you know what I noticed that first night? Nobody asked where the meat was. A hot skillet of potatoes with the eggs still soft on top, a cup of coffee, and the crew went quiet the way a crew only goes quiet when the food is good. That's when I knew we'd get through those two weeks.
[TWO] Supper number two. Cabbage and noodles. The Polish boys in the crew called it haluski, and the first time I made it Big Swede looked at the pot and said, that's it? Cabbage? Then he had three plates.
[TWO] One head of green cabbage. Two onions. Six tablespoons of butter, or half butter and half bacon fat. Twelve ounces of wide egg noodles. Salt and plenty of black pepper. If you have four slices of bacon or a little piece of sausage, good, but you don't need it.
[TWO] The whole secret of this supper is patience. Most people throw the cabbage in, it goes limp and watery in five minutes, and they call it done. And it tastes like wet cabbage. Nobody wants that.
[TWO] Melt the butter, cook the onions ten minutes. Then the cabbage goes in with the salt, and you cook it over medium for twenty, twenty-five minutes, turning it now and then, until it's soft and golden and brown at the edges. It goes sweet, friend. Almost like caramel.
[TWO] Meanwhile you boil the noodles in salty water and drain them. Toss them with the cabbage and a lot of pepper. A whole head of cabbage feeds the table, and the plate comes out around eighty cents. Low and slow until it's golden. That's the whole dish.
[TWO] One thing about the cabbage, friend. A head of cabbage keeps for weeks in a cool place. In the camp we kept them in the root cellar wrapped in newspaper, and in March they were still sweet. So when it's cheap at the store, buy two. It's the cheapest vegetable there is per pound, and it fills a plate like nothing else.
[THREE] Supper number three. This is the one the crew asked for by name on payday week. Hamburger gravy over potatoes. Toivo would come in the door, stamp the snow off, and holler, is it gravy night, Ole?
[THREE] One pound of ground beef. One onion. A quarter cup of flour. Two cups of milk and one cup of beef broth, and the broth can be water and a bouillon cube. Salt, and a full teaspoon of black pepper. Then mashed potatoes, or toast, or rice, whatever you've got.
[THREE] Here's what people get wrong. They drain all the fat off the beef because somebody told them fat is bad. But the fat is what makes the gravy. Brown the beef with the onion and leave about two tablespoons of fat in the pan.
[THREE] Sprinkle in the flour and stir it a full two minutes. Don't skip that. If you rush it, the gravy tastes like paste. Then pour the milk and the broth in slowly while you stir, and let it bubble five minutes until it's thick.
[THREE] One pound of beef feeds four people this way, because the gravy carries the meat over the potatoes. About a dollar ten a plate. And use a lot of pepper. This is a gravy that should bite back a little.
[THREE] And here's how I stretched it when I really had to. Half the beef and twice the onion, and a big pot of mashed potatoes underneath. The gravy still tastes like beef, because the browned bits in the pan are where the beef flavor lives. Toivo never noticed. Or if he noticed, he was smart enough not to say it to the cook.
[FOUR] Supper number four. Onion soup with cheese toast on top. This is the one for the coldest night, when the thermometer on the cook shack door said thirty below and nobody wanted to talk, they just wanted something hot.
[FOUR] Five big onions. Four tablespoons of butter. A teaspoon of salt and a teaspoon of sugar. Two tablespoons of flour. Six cups of beef broth, water and bouillon is fine. A bay leaf, a pinch of thyme. Four slices of bread, toasted, and a cup of any cheese that melts.
[FOUR] Now, the onions. Everybody rushes the onions. They cook them ten minutes and the soup comes out pale and sharp. You need forty-five minutes over medium low, stirring now and then, until they're deep brown.
[FOUR] And here's the trick Halvor's mother taught me. When the onions start to stick to the bottom of the pot, don't panic. Add a spoon of water and scrape. Every time you do that, the soup gets darker and better. That brown on the bottom of the pot is the flavor.
[FOUR] Then the flour two minutes, the broth, the bay leaf and the thyme, twenty minutes of simmer. Ladle it into bowls, a slice of toast on top, the cheese on the toast, and under the broiler two or three minutes until it bubbles. About ninety-five cents a bowl.

[FIVE] And supper number five. The cheapest one, and the one the crew liked best. Macaroni and tomatoes. My mother called it goulash, and in the camp nobody argued with her name for it.
[FIVE] One pound of elbow macaroni. Three tablespoons of butter. One onion. One big can of diced tomatoes. One tablespoon of sugar, salt, pepper. A cup of grated cheddar on top. That's all of it.
[FIVE] Here's the mistake. People cook the macaroni all the way first, then bake it, and it turns to mush. Pull it out of the water two minutes before the package says. It finishes in the oven and it holds its shape.
[FIVE] Cook the onion in the butter six minutes, add the tomatoes, the salt, the pepper and that spoon of sugar. The sugar takes the tinny edge off canned tomatoes. You won't taste sweet. You'll taste tomato. Simmer five minutes.
