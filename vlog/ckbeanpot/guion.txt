[STEP1] Step one is the soak. Put your beans in a big bowl with plenty of water and let them sit overnight. [STEP2] Step two is the boil. Put the drained beans in your pot, cover them with fresh water, and bring them to a rolling boil. Boil them hard for ten minutes. Then turn the heat down and let them simmer about forty-five minutes. Here is the part that matters most. You don't go by the clock. You go by the spoon. Drain the beans, but save the liquid in a jar, because you are going to need about two cups of it later. The liquid is full of the flavor of the beans, and it is a shame to pour it down the sink.
[TRICK] And now comes the trick. This is the one thing I want you to remember if you forget everything else. Take a spoonful of those beans, hold it up, and blow on it gently. When the skins curl back and split open, the beans are ready for the oven. I call it the blow test. Too early, and the beans stay hard no matter how long you bake them. Too late, and they fall apart into paste before they ever get to the table. Bernice taught me that test over a coffee cup in 1971, and I have never once needed a timer since.
[STEP3] Step three is the pot itself. Heat your oven to three hundred degrees Fahrenheit. [STEP4] Step four is the sauce, and this is where the sweetness comes from. In a bowl, stir together the molasses, the brown sugar, the dry mustard, the salt, the pepper and the ketchup. Then lay the rest of the salt pork on top, so it sits up where it can get brown and crisp.
