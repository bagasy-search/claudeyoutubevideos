[P9|] Rule number one. No overnight soak. You just sort 'em. Pour the dry beans out on a pan and go through 'em with your fingers. You're looking for little stones, dirt clods, and any shriveled or broken bean. I cracked a tooth on a pebble in nineteen sixty-three, and the whole crew heard about it for a week. Then rinse 'em in a colander under cold water. That's all the prep a bean needs.

[P11|] Rule number two. Beans go in the pot with cold water, two inches over the top, a halved onion, a bay leaf, some black pepper, a chunk of salt pork, and a tablespoon of salt. I'll tell you why the salt in a minute. And listen close on this one, because this isn't about taste. This one's about keeping you safe. You bring that pot up to a hard, rolling boil, and you hold it there a full ten minutes. That's the least. Kidney beans especially carry a natural poison when they're raw, and a real boil is what kills it. Four or five undercooked red beans can put a grown man on the floor for a night.
[P12|] That's why you never, ever put raw kidney beans in a slow cooker. A slow cooker doesn't get hot enough. The boil comes first, every time, every bean. Skim off that foam while it rolls.
[P13|] But here's the part folks get wrong the other way. They keep it rolling. For two hours. And they wonder why their beans split open and turn to mush.
[P14|] Rule number three. After those ten minutes, you drop it down to a whisper. Barely a bubble. One lazy bubble every second or two. Set the lid on crooked, so there's a crack. That crack is your throttle. A hard boil knocks those beans against each other and against the pot, and it tears the skins. A whisper lets 'em swell up slow and stay whole. An hour and a half, maybe two. Add hot water if the level drops below the beans.
[P15|] Now I'll tell you how you know when it's done. Take one bean out on a spoon and blow on it. If the skin curls back and peels, you're close. Then squeeze it against the roof of your mouth with your tongue. If it smashes easy, it's done. If it fights you, give it more time.
[P16|] Rule number four. Now, about that salt. It goes in at the start. This is the one that starts fights. The old rule was, you never salt a bean till it's soft, or it'll turn to rocks. That's backwards. Salt in the water softens the skin, and it seasons that bean clear to the center. You salt at the end, you get a bland middle every time. One tablespoon of kosher salt to a pound of beans, when the pot goes on.
[P17|] Rule number five. Put fat in the pot, right at the start too. A chunk of salt pork, a few strips of thick bacon, a ham hock, or a spoonful of lard. Fat carries the flavor, it gives the broth some body, and it makes the pot taste like it's been on the stove all day.
[P18|] And that brings me to rule number six. The one that ruins more pots than all the others together. Acid and sweet go in last. Tomato, vinegar, ketchup, mustard, molasses. They all make the skin tough and they stop the softening. Put 'em in at the start, and you can cook those beans all day long and they'll still be hard.
[P19|] I learned that one the hard way. My first winter, I dumped the molasses in with the dry beans to save time. Cooked 'em all day. Hard as buckshot at supper.
