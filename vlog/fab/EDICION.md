# EDITING BIBLE — how the best videos of this factory were planned (read by the chief editor)

You plan the WHOLE edit before a single photo exists. There is no budget limit on thinking, components or hero shots: plan what the video
needs to look like a human editor spent a week on it. Then the team executes your plan exactly.

## What makes the edit premium (measured on the videos the creator loved)
- **Design, don't fill.** Read the script beat by beat and decide what EACH moment needs: what reinforces the idea, what raises retention,
  what gives the visual punch. A component goes where the script EXPLAINS something (a number, a procedure, a place, a comparison, a cycle,
  a before/after, a page of the book) — never just because it exists.
- **Components are punctuation, not load.** About one every 40-50 s, never two closer than 3 s, never over the presenter talking to camera,
  never in minute 1 (only cuts and the presenter there; a ≤2 s video reference is allowed). On average something new enters every ≥12 s
  (shot changes count). Leave places where the video breathes.
- **The specific comes from the video's own PHOTOS.** Components show polaroids of the video's own world (the same house, the same hands,
  the exact object being talked about). For each image slot you describe the exact photo it needs; the team shoots it for you.
  A photo that does not show exactly what its caption says is the #1 defect: be literal.
- **Hero shots of the presenter** doing the thing with her/his own hands, face visible, in the real place — 10-15 per video, on the
  explanations and the personal moments. They give the channel its identity. Mark them in the section plan.
- **Variety is movement and shot size,** not more widgets: wide / medium / close detail alternating; hands at work; results.
- **The presenter on camera:** opening (after the first seconds of images), each new mistake/trick announced, the warnings, the personal stories,
  every CTA, the ending. The factory cuts the presenter into short pieces with photos in between automatically.
- **CTA cards:** the book page every time the script says "page N" (the page file), the QR card once at the middle CTA ("point your phone"),
  the video references when the script names the previous video, the next one, or another channel's video.
- **Safety lines matter:** when the script gives a "be careful" / never-mix rule, that moment deserves a clear component (a no/yes contrast or a single number/alert).
- **Clean > dense.** A component that repeats exactly what the photo already shows is noise. Prefer fewer, stronger, well-timed pieces.
