[ONE] Trick number one is picking the right roast. Not every chuck roast has the steak in it. The chuck is the shoulder of the cow, and it's a big part, a lot of different muscles. Down by the neck it's all hard work and gristle. But up at the other end, where the shoulder meets the ribs, there's a muscle that keeps on going right into the ribeye. It's the same muscle. The ribeye is just the next stop down the line.
[ONE] So when you're at the counter, turn the package over and look at the end of the roast. You're looking for one round eye of meat, about the size of your fist, with a thin ring of fat around it and fine little white flecks running all through it. That's the chuck eye. If you see that eye, buy that roast. If all you see is a crisscross of thick white seams and no eye, put it back. That one's for the stew pot.
[ONE] And don't be shy. Ask the person behind the counter, do you have a chuck roast from the rib end? A good meat cutter knows exactly what you mean. In my shop we called it the first cut. [TWO] Trick number two. Follow the seams with your knife. Put the roast on the board, eye side up. Now look close. Between the muscles there are thin white lines, like the borders on a map. That's where the cow is already split for you. You don't cut through the meat. You slide the tip of your knife into that seam and you just follow it, little strokes, and pull the muscle away with your other hand.
[TWO] Take your time here. Five minutes, no more. When you're done you'll have the eye, that round muscle that looks like a little ribeye, sitting on one side. And on the other side, the rest. [TWO] Now trim the eye. There's usually a hard yellow-white strip of gristle on the outside, the silver skin. That stuff never melts, not in a pan, not in a hundred years. [THREE] Trick number three. Cut it thick, and cut it across. Look at the meat. You'll see the fibers, the little lines running one direction, like the grain in a piece of wood.
