[T2] Trick number two is fresh air, even in winter. Open two windows on opposite sides of the house for ten minutes. Let the cold air come straight through and push the stale air out the other side. It feels silly to let the heat go, I know. But ten minutes with the windows open clears out more than an hour of closed-up air ever could, and you will feel the house breathe the moment the breeze crosses the room.
[T3] Trick number three is a bowl of baking soda, set in the room that smells. Put it on the table, on a windowsill, or on the floor by the chair. Baking soda pulls the odor in and holds it, and it does that quietly, without a single chemical hissing at you. If you have coffee grounds in a bowl instead, that works too, and the coffee smell is a good smell in a house. Keep the bowl out of reach of the little ones and the dog, and change the baking soda every few weeks so it keeps doing its job.
[T4] Trick number four is the trash and the sink disposal. Take the trash out, every single day in the summer, before it has a chance to get ripe. Then run the disposal with a strip of lemon peel while the water runs cold. The lemon freshens the blades and leaves a bright little scent behind, and the sink stops smelling like last night's fish. That is one of the places a smell likes to hide, and we will come back to that.
[T5] Trick number five is the refrigerator shelves. Once a month, take each shelf out one at a time and wipe it with warm water and two tablespoons of baking soda per quart. Pull the drawers out and wash them in the sink, but wait until they come to room temperature first. Cold glass in hot water cracks, honey. [T6] Trick number six is the rubber door seal, where the black mold hides in the folds. Spray the seal with three percent hydrogen peroxide and let it sit for a minute. Then scrub every fold with an old toothbrush, and wipe it dry with a clean rag. Keep the peroxide in its brown bottle, out of the light, and it will keep working for you a good long time.
[T7] Trick number seven is the range hood filter. Pop the metal filter out of the hood and set it in the sink. Pour boiling water over it, careful now, and sprinkle a quarter cup of baking soda on top. Squirt a little blue dish soap, let it soak for fifteen minutes, then brush and rinse. The grease melts right off in the hot water. You barely have to brush at all, and the fan starts pulling the smoke out the way it should.
[T8] Trick number eight is for towels that smell sour the second they get damp. It is the soap and the softener, building up in the cloth and holding the smell like a sponge holds a stain. Wash the towels in the hottest water they allow, with one cup of white vinegar and no detergent at all. Yes, no detergent. Then they are ready for the next step, and they will feel rough for a day, which is the old soap leaving.
[T9] Trick number nine finishes the job. Wash the towels hot again, this time with half a cup of baking soda and no detergent. Dry them completely, right away, and never leave wet towels sitting in the washer, not even for an hour. From now on, use half the detergent, skip the fabric softener, and put the vinegar in the rinse. Your towels will be soft in a new way, and they will not sour on you. If the towels still smell after the second wash, check that your washer drum is not holding wet detergent in the seal, and wipe it dry.
[T10] Trick number ten is the rubber ring on a front-loading washer. Pull back the ring and look in the fold, and you will see the gunk. Spray it with peroxide and wait ten minutes. [T11] Trick number eleven is the dryer lint screen. Clean it after every load, and once a month wash it in warm soapy water, because the softener leaves a film that holds the smell. [T12] Trick number twelve is the carpet that smells musty, or like the dog. Vacuum first. Then shake baking soda evenly over the carpet, and leave it for an hour.
