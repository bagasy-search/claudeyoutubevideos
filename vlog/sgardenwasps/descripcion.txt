Exterminators charge $400 to spray a wasp nest. My Japanese mother kept wasps away from her little house in Osaka for sixty years with three things from the kitchen: a bottle of soapy water, a small jar of cloves and a brown paper bag. No poison, never once. Here is exactly what she did, with every measure, the hour of the day it works, and the honest part: when you should NOT do this yourself.

THE 3 THINGS
1. Soapy water: 2 tablespoons of plain dish soap in 1 quart of water. A straight stream, not a mist. Only at dusk, when the wasps are home and too cold to fly. Soak the nest until it drips, walk away calmly, knock it into a bucket of soapy water the next evening and wash the spot. Only small open nests, smaller than your fist, that you can reach from the ground.
2. Cloves: 1 tablespoon of crushed cloves in a little cloth bag in each corner of the porch, squeezed every 2 weeks, new every month. Clove water: 2 tablespoons of cloves simmered 15 minutes in 2 cups of water, plus 1 teaspoon of soap, sprayed once a week in spring. Keep it in the refrigerator, make it new every 2 weeks. And half a lemon with 10 to 12 cloves on the picnic table.
3. The paper bag: stuffed to the size of a grapefruit, twisted, tied with a string and crumpled so it looks gray and old. Hung in the shade in early spring, when the forsythia blooms, before the queen chooses her room. Keep it dry.

SAFETY
Never treat a nest in the ground, a closed gray football-shaped nest, or wasps going into a wall. If anyone in the house is allergic, or if a sting is on the face or throat, call for help right away. Honeybees are not wasps: call a beekeeper.
