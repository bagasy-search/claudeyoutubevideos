[R51_ING] Here is what you need for Make-Ahead Church Mashed Potatoes, which serves twelve. Five pounds of russet or gold potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks. One tablespoon of salt for the cooking water. Eight ounces of cream cheese, softened. One cup of sour cream. Half a cup of butter, plus two tablespoons more for the top. Half a cup of warm milk. One and a half teaspoons of salt, half a teaspoon of pepper, and one teaspoon of onion powder. And paprika and chopped chives to finish. Write it down, honey, or keep the book open by the flour.
[R51_S1] Step one. Put the potatoes in a big pot with cold water and the tablespoon of salt. Start them in cold water, not hot, and bring them up to a boil together. Then let them simmer fifteen to twenty minutes, until a fork slides right into the middle of a piece with no fight at all. Starting them cold means the potatoes cook through evenly, so the outside is not mush while the center is still hard.
[R51_S2] Step two. Drain the potatoes well, and then let them steam dry in the hot pot for two minutes. This is the step people skip, and it is why their potatoes come out watery. Every drop of water you leave in the pot ends up in the mash, and wet mashed potatoes will not hold a shape on a plate. Two minutes of steam is all it takes to drive that water out of them.
[R51_S3] Step three. Mash the potatoes with the cream cheese, the sour cream, the half cup of butter and the warm milk. Then season them with the salt, the pepper and the onion powder, and taste a spoonful before you go on. Warm milk goes into the potatoes more easily than cold, and it keeps them smooth. Use a hand masher or give them a quick beat, and do not beat them for long. Too much beating turns mashed potatoes gluey, and that is the mistake I see most.
[R51_S4] Step four. Spread the potatoes in a buttered nine by thirteen pan. Dot the top with the two tablespoons of butter, and dust the surface with paprika so it looks inviting in the refrigerator light. Cover the pan, put it in the refrigerator, and it will keep up to two days. That means a Tuesday mashed potato dish is still perfectly good for Thursday dinner, as long as it has been covered the whole time.
[R51_S5] Step five is the Thursday step. Take the pan out of the refrigerator about thirty minutes before you bake it, so the middle is not cold. Bake it covered at three hundred fifty degrees for thirty minutes. Then take off the cover, and bake ten to fifteen minutes more, until the top is golden and the center reads one hundred sixty-five degrees on your thermometer. Sprinkle the chives on at the table. That is how you go from a covered dish in the refrigerator to a golden potato dish in front of your guests.
[R51_TRICK] Now, the trick, the one line I would want you to remember if you forget everything else. It is the cream cheese and the sour cream. They are what keep the potatoes creamy after a night in the refrigerator, instead of gluey and gray. My friend Irene used to say a potato is only as good as the fat you give it. She was right, and the cream cheese and the sour cream are the fat that carries this dish through two days in the cold.
[R51_MISTAKES] Let me tell you the three mistakes I have seen over and over. The first is gluey potatoes, from too much beating in a mixer or with a blender. Use a hand masher, or a quick beat. The second is watery potatoes, from skipping that two minutes of steam in the pot. The third is a cold center, from going straight from the refrigerator into the oven. Thirty minutes on the counter first, and you will not have that problem at all.
