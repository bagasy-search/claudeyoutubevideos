Or they close the hole in the fall and forget that the squirrel comes back at the end of winter to look at it again. So every year, in December, before the babies, I walk around my house once with a cup of tea and look at every door. Ten minutes. And every year, the squirrels look at my roof, they find every door closed, and they go back to the oak tree, where they belong.
[RECAP] So, one more time, very fast. Red chili, to teach the squirrel that your garden is bad food. Dish soap, one teaspoon, so the chili stays where the squirrel bites. And peppermint, changed every three days, to make the attic uncomfortable. Daffodils and onions around the tulips. Water dish and early tomatoes in summer. No poison, no traps. And close the door last, after the guest is gone, before February.
[CLOSE] My mother never saw an American squirrel. She died in Osaka when she was ninety one, and the last time I visited, I told her about the oak tree and the tomatoes, and she laughed so much. She said, you see, Sachiko, the animal is not the problem. The open door is the problem. I still have her letter in the tin box. Blue paper. Three words and one line.
